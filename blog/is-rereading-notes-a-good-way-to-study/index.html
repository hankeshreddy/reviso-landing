Is re-reading your notes a waste of time?

Dobara padhna vs khud se yaad karna: what the research says.

Reviso team4 Nov 20262 min read

⚡ Short answer

Re-reading is not useless, but it is one of the weakest ways to remember things for an exam. It feels productive because the material looks familiar. Methods that make you pull information out of memory (active recall) and spread practice over days (spaced repetition) work much better for the same time spent.

Why re-reading feels good but works badly

The second time you read a page, it flows smoothly. That smoothness feels like learning. But familiarity is not the same as being able to answer a question with the book closed. A large review of study techniques rated re-reading and highlighting as low-usefulness methods, while practice testing and spaced practice were rated highly (Dunlosky and colleagues, 2013).

What to do instead

Active recall

After reading a section, close the book and ask yourself: what were the main points? Write them, say them, or answer questions on them. Then check. Every attempt, even a wrong one, makes the memory stronger than another read.

Spaced repetition

Do not test yourself on a topic five times in one evening. Do it once today, again after a few days, and again after a week or two. Spreading practice out leads to much better long-term memory than cramming the same amount of practice together (Cepeda and colleagues, 2006).

Questions students ask

🤔Is highlighting useless?

Highlighting on its own adds little. It helps only if you later turn the highlighted points into questions and test yourself.

⏳What is active recall?

Pulling information out of your memory without looking, for example answering questions or writing notes from memory, and then checking.

🗣️What is spaced repetition?

Reviewing the same material at growing gaps, such as after 1 day, 3 days, 7 days and 2 weeks, instead of all at once.
